# Dictation: voice only, pasted where you type

Read this before changing `src/dictation/` (`chord.rs`, the state machine; `session.rs`, the runtime; `commands.rs`, spoken edit commands; `context.rs`, filenames and code; `format.rs` and `formatter.rs`, paragraphs and lists; `learn.rs`, words learned from edits; `layout.rs`, layouts learned from edits), `src/dictionary.rs` or the dictation pill.

Dictation is the screenless sibling of a capture: a modifier-only chord records the microphone, the configured transcriber turns it into text, and Scrawl pastes the text into the app in front. It is off by default (`"dictation": true` turns it on, or Settings › Dictation).

## The chord (`dictation::Chord`)

The default chord is `⌃⌥` (`dictationShortcut`, also `⌃⌘`, `⌥⌘` or `⌃⇧`). It is a modifier-only chord, so Carbon cannot register it. The UI loop polls `capture::modifier_flags()` (no permission needed) and feeds `Chord::tick`, and the key tap's key-down counter (`SharedCaptureTelemetry::key_downs`, Accessibility) tells it when the modifiers were the start of an ordinary shortcut. With the key tap off (no Accessibility, or Secure Input on, as in a password field) dictation doesn't start. The clipboard is not restored when it held a password manager's concealed or transient item.

- **Hold to talk.** The chord goes down: the microphone starts at once, so the first word is kept. Held past `TAP_MS`, the pill shows and the command transcriber prestarts. Release: transcribe and paste.
- **Double tap to latch.** Two taps within `DOUBLE_TAP_MS`: hands-free. The pill shows ✕ and ✓. The chord again (pressed and released with no key in between) or ✓ confirms and pastes; Esc or ✕ discards. Any dictation stops itself after `chord::MAX_MS`.
- **Not dictation.** A key press, a mouse button, or another modifier joining, while the chord is down and not latched, discards the recording silently: `⌃⌥←`, `⌃⌥⇧S` or a `⌃⌥`-drag stays what it was. A single short tap is discarded too. Recordings shorter than `MIN_AUDIO_MS` are not transcribed, and a transcript with no letters or digits is not pasted.
- The microphone opens on every press of the chord, so the menu bar's microphone indicator flickers on `⌃⌥` shortcuts; a discarded recorder is dropped off the UI thread.
- Dictation never starts while a capture is recording, selecting or processing. The capture shortcut cancels a held, unlatched chord (a capture shortcut may contain its modifiers) and is ignored while dictation is latched or pasting.

## The pill

Dictation shares Scrawl's one capsule with captures (`ScrawlCoordinator::tick_dictation`). While no capture is on it, dictation gets its hover and clicks (`PillInput`) and decides what it shows (`session::presentation`); a capture takes it over and gives it back when its island is hidden. The capsule sits at the bottom centre of the active screen (`screens::active_point`) and grows upward from there, as Wispr Flow's pill does. With a Dock that stays shown at the bottom it sits just above it; with a hidden Dock (or one at the side) it sits a few points above the screen's own bottom edge, where it stays in front of the Dock when the Dock pops up (`bottom_edge`). It needs the native capsule (`nativePill`, on by default on macOS):

- `Pres::DictationIdle`: a small see-through bar with a light rim (`FrameState::is_idle`), on while dictation and `dictationPill` (default on) both are. Hovered, it offers Dictate and Record.
- Hovered, `Pres::Hint`: "Dictate ⌃⌥", which starts hands-free dictation (`Chord::latch`), Record, which does what a tap of the record shortcut does, and, once there is a dictation to copy, Copy (`ElemId::CopyLast`, "Copy last dictation"), which puts the last dictation on the clipboard again and says "Copied".
- `Pres::Dictating { latched }`: the dot, the clock and the mic level line, with ✕ and ✓ when latched; `Pres::Processing` while it transcribes.
- The mic level line (`native/pill/wave.rs`) shows the mic is hearing. `AudioRecorder::rms` is the RMS of the newest 32 ms. The line spans 34 dB above a floor of −52 dBFS, or 4 dB above the room's noise when that is louder (the quietest recent level, rising 6 dB/s through speech), so steady noise draws flat. It rises in 40 ms and falls over 140 ms; the newest level sits at the centre and older ones ripple outward, and the line fades into the capsule at both ends. The Metal composite pass draws it after the clock, so it fades, scales and blurs with the lead. The always-on idle bar never shows it. A flat line draws no new frames. A screen capture that records the Mac's mic shows the same line in its lead (`Pres::Recording { mic }`, `capture_mic_rms`); a paused capture, or an iPhone one (its narration comes from the phone), doesn't. `scrawl lab pill dictation` and the lab's pretend recordings feed a pretend voice.
- A notice, `Pres::Message` with a check, for `notices::NOTICE_FOR` (3.5 s) from when it shows: "Copied to the clipboard" when nothing was focused and "Didn't catch that" when nothing was heard. With **Show what dictation learns** (`dictationNotices`, default on) it also says how the formatter laid text out ("Formatted as a list", "Split into paragraphs"; `format::describe`) and what an edit taught: "Added “Klose” to your dictionary" for each word, "“shawn” will be written “Sean”" for each replacement, and "Learned your formatting" for a layout. Those carry **Undo** (`ElemId::Undo`, `PillAction::Undo`), which removes the word or the replacement (recording the word, or what the replacement wrote, in `learnedRejected`, so it is never learned again) or the layout example. A click on the text of a learned or layout notice (`Notice::opens`, a `settings::Focus`) opens Settings › Dictation there: at the word or replacement, scrolled to, lit for a moment and open for editing, or at the learned-layouts row for "Learned your formatting", "Formatted as a list" and "Split into paragraphs". Such a notice lights more on hover, with an underline (`NoticeKind::Opens`); a click on any other notice only dismisses it. Notices queue (`dictation::notices`, at most six waiting) and show one at a time. One that arrives while the user dictates or a capture holds the pill waits, and a notice with Undo that the pill gave up partway shows again in full; one without Undo that already showed goes.

Every state sits at one window level, above the Dock and the menu bar and above Scrawl's overlays (`WINDOW_LEVEL`), on every Space and over full-screen apps, and lets clicks through outside the capsule. While a capture records, selects or processes, the capsule shows the capture and dictation's chord is ignored; recordings leave the capsule out because SCK excludes Scrawl's windows above layer 0, and the capsule's level is. Element tiles are packed by width (`render_frame`), so a message wider than a chip isn't cut off. `scrawl lab pill dictation` plays these states.

## Transcription and paste

`session::transcribe_and_paste` writes the WAV to a temp file and runs `transcribe::transcribe`, the same engines and filler removal as captures (the command transcriber's prestart applies). `dictionary::Dictionary::apply` then fixes words, and `session::deliver` asks Accessibility whether the focused element takes text (`ax::focused_takes_text`: a text role, or a selection on an element whose `AXValue` can be set; read-only selectable text doesn't count). If it does, `handoff::paste_text` puts the text on the clipboard, posts `⌘V`, and puts the previous clipboard back after `RESTORE_DELAY_MS` (an empty clipboard stays empty) unless something else changed it meanwhile (`dictationRestoreClipboard`, default on). Every route pastes, since an app with its Accessibility tree off can report nothing focused over a real field. Before a paste into a field, `session::fitted` reads the selection (`AXSelectedTextRange`) and up to two characters on each side of it (`AXStringForRange`, else `AXValue`; `ax::FocusedText::around_insertion`), and `spacing::fit` fits the text to them, as Wispr Flow does. Text that starts with a word gets a space in front when it would land right after a word or punctuation, so a second dictation into one message reads "dictation? Also"; the field's start, whitespace, an opening bracket or quote, and a selection (which the paste replaces) get none. Text pasted right after an opening `(`, `[`, `{` or `“` gets the closer at its end ("Notes (see below.)"), unless text follows the caret directly (it already belongs to the bracket), the field has a closer after the caret, the text closes it itself, or the text after the caret can't be read. A field that can't be read at all (some web and Electron editors) gets the text unchanged. When the system says nothing is focused (`kAXErrorNoValue`), the text also stays on the clipboard and the pill says "Copied to the clipboard". An app that doesn't answer in time, or a focused element whose role doesn't say, gets the paste and keeps the text on the clipboard, so nothing is lost. A click on the hint just after a notice goes (`CLICK_GRACE`) is taken as meant for the notice. Playback ducks while dictating, as during a capture, but over 0.12 s (`playback::Guard::start_quick`) instead of a capture's one-second fade, so it is quiet before the first word.

## Recent dictations (`src/dictation/history.rs`)

Every dictation pasted or copied is kept as the last, in memory, for the pill's Copy: one lost to a clipboard that something else overwrote (a screenshot straight after) can be copied again. With **Keep recent dictations** (`dictationHistory`, default on) the newest `MAX_ENTRIES` (20) also go to `~/Library/Application Support/scrawl/dictation-history.json`, owner-only, so a quit or crash keeps them, and the menu bar's **Recent Dictations** lists the newest `MENU_ENTRIES` (10): each item is the first line, cut short, and its age, and copies its dictation. The text is as pasted, after filler removal, the dictionary and formatting. The last recording's audio is kept too, owner-only, in `last-dictation.wav` beside the history and overwritten by each dictation, whatever came of it: one that came back with no speech or failed to paste is the one most worth checking, so this file can be newer than the last kept text; each dictation also logs where its measured word times end against the audio's length ("transcript ends at 41.2 s of 62.7 s"). **Clear** in Settings › Dictation deletes both files and forgets the last; turning the setting off stops writing them but keeps what is there until Clear.

## Words spoken and speed (`src/word_stats.rs`)

Scrawl counts the words the user speaks into it and how fast. Each dictation pasted or copied counts its words after filler removal, the dictionary, spoken commands and the filename pass, and before the formatter's layout, whose list markers aren't words (`word_stats::count_words`: a run with a letter or digit). Each capture the app builds counts its transcript's words (`word_stats::record_capture`): a screen or cabled iPhone capture (`ui.rs`) and a ScrawlKit recording the app takes in (`IngestHooks::default`). `scrawl record`, `scrawl reprocess` and `scrawl import` count nothing, so trying a bundle again never counts it twice.

Speed counts only time spent talking (`word_stats::speaking_ms`). With measured word timings each word counts in full and each gap between two words counts up to `PAUSE_ALLOWANCE_MS` (1 s), so a breath counts and a pause to think doesn't: the user doesn't have to let go of the chord to keep the number honest. The cap is continuous, so a pause just over the allowance counts the same as one just under. Estimated timings (Hex) and transcripts without timings count the recording's length.

- `~/Library/Application Support/scrawl/word-stats.json`, owner-only, holds the totals for dictation and for captures and when counting began. It holds numbers, never text, so **Clear** (recent dictations) leaves it.
- Settings › Dictation's **Speaking** group shows the words spoken and the average speed over both, with **Reset** (`reset-word-stats`), which deletes the file, and **Show the speed after each dictation** (`dictationWpm`, default on).
- With that on, the pill says a dictation's speed after its paste ("152 WPM"), joined to a layout notice when there is one ("Formatted as a list  ·  152 WPM"). A dictation under `MIN_NOTICE_WORDS` (8) words says none, since a few words make a noisy number. Copied and failed dictations say only what they said before.
- The menu bar menu shows "12,480 words  ·  148 WPM" above **Recent Dictations**, read fresh each time it opens, and hides the line before the first word.

## Dictionary (`src/dictionary.rs`)

`~/.config/scrawl/dictionary.json`, read on each dictation:

```json
{
  "words": ["Ferris", "Varde Labs", "Parakeet"],
  "replacements": { "feris": "Ferris", "var de labs": "Varde Labs" }
}
```

- `replacements` match whole words, ignoring case, longest first.
- `words` fix casing and spacing (`varde labs`, `vardelabs` → `Varde Labs`) and near misses of words of 7 letters or more (one edit, two from 10 letters, same first letter). Shorter words have real neighbours (`ferries` and `Ferris`), so they need a replacement.

Parakeet takes no vocabulary prompt, so the dictionary corrects after transcription.

## Spoken commands (`src/dictation/commands.rs`)

Behind the experiment `spokenCommands` (off by default; see Experiments in `AGENTS.md`). After the dictionary and before the filename and code pass, `commands::apply` acts on what the speaker said to the text, not into it:

- "comma" and "question mark" write `,` and `?`, and "period" writes `.`. The spoken mark replaces a comma or full stop the transcriber put just before it.
- "new line" and "new paragraph" break the text, and the next word starts with a capital.
- "scratch that" drops the text back to the last comma, colon, sentence end or line break, so "I like apples, and oranges, scratch that, and pears" is "I like apples, and pears".
- "at the rate X" writes `@X`. A domain joins the word before it ("sam at the rate example.com" is `sam@example.com`). "at the rate of" and "at the rate we're going" stay prose.

A command matches only as whole words at a phrase boundary, so prose stays as spoken:

- A word joined to another ("comma-separated", "new-line-delimited") is a different word.
- After an article, a determiner or a possessive ("a", "the", "this", "no", "any", "every", "each", "another", "my", "our", "your", "his", "her", "its", "their") or "Oxford", the words name the thing ("a comma", "no comma", "our new line of products"). Other prose uses of "comma", "question mark", "new line" and "new paragraph" still read as commands, which is why the pass is an experiment.
- A command never spans the transcriber's sentence break ("Any question? Mark said no").
- "scratch that" is everyday prose ("scratch that itch"), so it counts only where what follows sets it off: its own punctuation ("scratch that,"), a capitalised next word, or the end of the dictation.
- "period" is everyday prose too ("the trial period"), and the transcriber ends almost every dictation with a full stop, so being set off says nothing about it. It counts when it stands alone between phrase ends ("Done. Period."), or, set off by what follows as above, when the same dictation also has a spoken "comma", "question mark", "new line" or "new paragraph", which shows the speaker is dictating punctuation. "We extended the trial period." keeps its last word.

Text with no command comes back byte for byte.

## Filenames and code (`src/dictation/context.rs`)

With **Format code and filenames** (`dictationCodeFormatting`, default on), the text goes through three pure steps after the dictionary and spoken commands, so a rule for "design dot md" still sees what was said. Prose is the common case, so every rule needs a spoken "dot" or a written `.` or `_`. Names (diFranco), brand names (iPhone, Node), camelCase, and phrasal verbs that match an identifier on screen ("sign in" beside `signIn`) all stay as spoken.

1. `spoken_extensions`: a lower-case spoken "dot" before a known extension joins the word before it, so "design dot MD" becomes "design.md". The name "Dot", "the", "a" or "an" before the "dot", and extensions that are everyday words or letters (`AMBIGUOUS`: go, c, h, sh, env, lock) are left as spoken.
2. `apply` with screen filenames: when the chord is released, `ax::window_text` walks the focused window's Accessibility tree breadth first, on a thread of its own alongside transcription. It stops at `SCREEN_ELEMENTS` elements or `SCREEN_BUDGET`, and never reads a secure text field. The paste waits for it at most `SCREEN_WAIT` after the chord; an app slow to answer Accessibility gets its text without filenames. `terms` keeps the filenames it finds. A spoken run of up to `MAX_SPAN_WORDS` words that ends in a spoken "dot" and an extension, and equals a screen filename on letters and digits (a possessive "'s" or "s'" dropped from the words before the extension), is written as that filename. So "the product's design dot MD" with `PRODUCT-DESIGN.md` on screen becomes `PRODUCT-DESIGN.md`, while "note text" beside `note.txt` stays.
3. Backticks go around filenames (not a product name such as `Node.js`) and `snake_case` (outside existing backticks, with punctuation and quotes outside), except in prose apps (`PROSE_APPS`: Mail, Messages, Notes, Pages, Word, Outlook).

With the setting off, none of this runs and nothing is read from the screen. Screen text is kept for one dictation only, and never stored or sent.

## Paragraphs and lists (`src/dictation/format.rs`, `formatter.rs`)

A long dictation is laid out as Wispr Flow lays one out: paragraphs where the topic changes, and numbered or bulleted lists when the speaker lists things. A local model does the layout (`scripts/scrawl-format.py`, a 4-bit Qwen2.5 3B through mlx-lm, set up like Parakeet). Nothing leaves the Mac. It needs `"formatCommand": ["/Users/you/.local/share/uv/tools/mlx-lm/bin/python", "/path/to/scrawl-format.py"]`, and **Format paragraphs and lists** (`dictationFormatting`, default on).

1. Once a dictation has run `FORMAT_PRESTART_AFTER` (5 s), `formatter::Formatter::prestart` takes the parked formatter (below) or starts one with `SCRAWL_PRESTART=1`, so its model loads while the user talks. A cancelled dictation drops it, which kills its process group. While it lays text out, the dictation counts as busy, as transcription does.
2. After filler removal, the dictionary, spoken commands and the filename and code pass, `format::worth_formatting` sends only dictations of `MIN_WORDS` words and `MIN_SENTENCES` sentences or more. Shorter ones park the formatter unused.
3. The request carries the text and `format::pause_hints`: the sentences after which the transcriber's measured word timings show a silence of `PARAGRAPH_PAUSE_MS` or more (estimated timings, as Hex's, give no hints). The paste waits for the reply for `FORMAT_WAIT_BASE` plus `FORMAT_WAIT_PER_WORD` per word, at most `FORMAT_WAIT_MAX`. When the model returned the text with no line breaks, `break_at_pauses` breaks at those pauses; once it laid the text out, its sentence ends may have moved, so the hints no longer apply.
4. `format::same_words` checks that the reply has the dictated words in order. It ignores the punctuation layout changes around a word (`,` `.` `:` `;`, double quotes, brackets) and line-leading list markers. A word's first letter may change case only where a sentence, line or list item starts, so a name or "I" keeps its capital elsewhere; code in backticks must match exactly (`README.md` never becomes `readme.md`). Markdown the model adds (`**`, `_`, backticks, a fence) counts as a change. Characters inside a word (`1.5`, `$50`, `We're`), apostrophes and a word's `?` or `!` must match. A dictated "and" or "or" may be missing only right before a list's last item. A sentence ending inside closing quotes (`"stop."`) counts as a sentence end. Any other change, a timeout or a bad reply pastes the text as dictated. The log says which happened, never the text.

5. The prompt carries up to `layout::MAX_EXAMPLES` layouts learned from the user's edits (below) as examples after the built-in one, so the model's layout drifts toward the user's. The formatter reads them from `SCRAWL_FORMAT_EXAMPLES` at prestart and computes the prompt up to the dictation while the user talks, so they add no wait at paste time. The pause hints follow as a system note of their own. `same_words` holds a reply to the dictated words as strictly as before.

Between dictations the models stay loaded (`crate::park`). After each layout a fresh formatter is started and parked, and a formatter a dictation didn't need goes back to the park. After each dictation's layout, `transcribe::park_transcriber` starts and parks the next transcriber too. The next dictation takes them only if they run the same command and are still running, and takes the formatter only if the learned layouts haven't changed since it loaded them. So a short dictation skips the transcriber's load and first pass, about 2.6 s for Parakeet. A parked process is dropped after `PARK_FOR` (10 minutes) unused. Until then it holds its model's memory, about 1.3 GB for Parakeet and about 2 GB for the formatter.

## Learning from edits (`src/dictation/learn.rs`)

With **Learn from my edits** (`dictationLearn`, default on), a paste starts `watch_edits` on a thread of its own:

1. `PASTE_SETTLE` after the paste, it holds the focused element (`ax::FocusedText`, never a secure text field, and never one over `MAX_FIELD_CHARS`, asked before its text is copied, with each read under the 0.1 s Accessibility timeout). It reads the element's `AXValue` and finds the paste in it (`learn::locate`, keeping `ANCHOR_CHARS` of text on each side, plus where the paste started, so a prefix that repeats is matched at the right copy). A paste it can't find is not watched.
2. Every `LEARN_EVERY` it reads the span between the anchors again (`edited_span`) into a `learn::Settle`, which keeps the last span once reads agree on it: two in a row, or three when the watch ends with the span gone (a chat message sent), since a word finished and sent within one read was never read whole. However it ends, the watch reads the element once more, an interval after the last read. The watch ends when focus leaves the element, after `LEARN_FOR` (60 s), or once the span can't be found or is empty, as when a chat message is sent. It then uses the settled span. A span unchanged for `AGREEING_WHILE_FOCUSED` reads (`Settle::quiet`, about 3 s) teaches at once, while the field keeps focus, so the user sees what was learned without leaving it; `Taught` keeps the watch from sending the same word or layout again. A word fixed, left alone that long, then changed again teaches both spellings; Undo in the pill takes back the first.
3. `learned` aligns the pasted and edited words (without case) and proposes short swaps that respell the old words: up to `MAX_HEARD_WORDS` words replaced by up to `MAX_TERM_WORDS`, spelled alike within `MAX_DISTANCE_RATIO`, with different letters (not just case), no digits and no `@`, and not a stretched word ("heyyy") or a vowel-dropped shortcut ("abt", "btwn"). A possessive "'s" comes off, and so does Markdown wrapping (`` ` ``, `*`, `_`, `~`), so backticking or bolding a word is no change; a `/` (a path) is never a term. The term is the run of new words that best matches what was heard, never more words than were heard, so text typed after a paste-final fix ("Pipedrive today") is left out. It proposes nothing when less than `MIN_KEPT` of the paste is kept or respelled, since that is a different text.
4. On the UI thread, `take_learned` keeps only proposals with a word the system spell checker doesn't know in English or in its own language (`SPELL_LANGUAGES`, always explicit, since automatic identification calls "teh" correct), and that it doesn't correct to a word one edit or one swapped pair of letters away (`spell_checker_typo`: "calender", "recieved"). A correction back to the word that was pasted counts too (the user's slip, "schedule" retyped as "schedyle"), unless the user changed its first letter (`learn::chose_name_over`): "Klose" corrects to "Close", and the user chose Klose over the pasted "close". That is what separates a name or term (Klose, Pipedrive, Obsbot) from ordinary editing ("The" to "This", "follow up" to "follow-up", "lets" to "let's"). Kept words go into `dictionary.json` with `Dictionary::learn_word`: into `words`, and into `learnedWords`, which Settings marks with ✨, at most `MAX_LEARNED` at a time. A word in `words` fixes a near miss only from 7 letters (`dictionary::near`), so a short name the transcriber mishears ("Shawn" for Sean) is learned as a replacement instead (`learns_as_replacement`, `Dictionary::learn_replacement`): one word each way, not a near miss of a word already in `words` (a long name the spell checker knows, "Johnathan" for Jonathan, is never learned as a word, so it gets a replacement), not a typo, and never a common word, since "close" respelled Klose would change every later "close". The spell checker tells them apart in lower case (`common_word`), where it knows "close" and "the" but not "shawn" or "jon". So Jon respelled John is learned too, as the user's choice of name, while The to This is not. Removing a learned word in Settings records it in `learnedRejected`, and it is never learned again (adding it by hand still works). Turning the setting off drops words still on their way. `dictionary::edit_lock` keeps this and Settings from saving over each other, and a dictionary that doesn't parse is left alone. The pill then shows a notice.

5. A paste the formatter was asked to lay out is also checked for a new layout (`layout::example`): with the respellings `learned` found applied to both sides, the settled span must differ from the paste in line breaks, list markers or punctuation and keep the dictated words exactly as `same_words` holds the formatter to them. Any other change of words, or a dictation over `MAX_EXAMPLE_WORDS`, teaches nothing. A span that vanished counts once it settled as for words (`AGREEING_WHEN_GONE` reads), so a prompt laid out and then sent teaches its layout; a field the user cleared after an edit reads the same and teaches it too. On the UI thread, with the formatter on, `layout::remember` adds the pair (what went to the formatter, the user's layout) to `format-examples.json` beside the dictionary, replacing an example of the same dictation, and drops the oldest past `MAX_EXAMPLES` or `MAX_TOTAL_CHARS`.

The field's text stays in memory for that one paste. Apart from recent dictations (below), only learned words are written, to `dictionary.json` on this Mac, and learned layouts, whole dictations as the user laid them out, to `format-examples.json` (owner-only), which **Forget** in Settings deletes; a `dictionary.json` symlinked into a dotfiles repository receives the learned words too (`format-examples.json` stays beside `config.json` and never follows the link), so turn learning off if names from other people's messages shouldn't land there.

## Settings › Dictation

The pane (`settings::DictationView`, `dictation` in `helper/Settings.swift`) holds the switch, the chord, **Always show the pill** (`dictationPill`), **Restore the clipboard after a paste**, **Format code and filenames** (`dictationCodeFormatting`, default on), **Format paragraphs and lists** (`dictationFormatting`, default on; dimmed until `formatCommand` is set up), **Learn from my edits** (`dictationLearn`, default on), **Show what dictation learns** (`dictationNotices`, default on), **Keep recent dictations** (`dictationHistory`, default on) with **Clear** (`clear-dictations`), the **Speaking** group (above), the count of learned layouts with **Forget** (`forget-layouts`), **Remove filler words** (`removeFillers`, shared with recordings) and the dictionary editor. `add-word`, `remove-word`, `edit-word`, `add-replacement`, `remove-replacement` and `edit-replacement` load, change and save `dictionary.json` (`Dictionary::save_to` renames a temp file into place and keeps keys it doesn't know). A double click on a word or replacement edits it in place; Return or a click elsewhere saves, and Esc cancels. An empty entry, or one with the words of another (ignoring case and spacing), is refused under the row as it is saved, and the row stays open; Scrawl checks again on save (`Dictionary::edit_word`, `edit_replacement`) and says "Not saved: …" under the list if the pane's check missed it. An edited learned word keeps its ✨ only while its text is unchanged, and its old spelling isn't recorded in `learnedRejected`. A `show` message with a `focus` (`{"kind":"word","entry":…}` or `{"kind":"layouts"}`) is how a pill notice opens the pane at an entry. A file that doesn't parse is shown as an error and never overwritten; **Edit as Text…** (`edit-dictionary`) opens it in the default text editor, made empty first if there is none. The pane is macOS only: the Windows settings window doesn't list it. Render it with `scrawl-helper settings --render DIR` (`dictation`, `dictation-empty`, `dictation-replacements`, `dictation-broken`, and, scrolled to the lists, `dictation-editing`, `dictation-edit-refused`, `dictation-editing-replacement` and `dictation-opened`).
